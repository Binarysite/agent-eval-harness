agent's `sd-01`), 23 `pass`. "Majority" is the verdict most of the five trials gave. Brackets

Rules alone, the scenario's `expect` block, catch 1 of the 25 bad replies (the regressed
agent's verbatim leak). Rules then the judge's majority, which is what the harness does,
catch 24 of 25 with either judge, and let through 21 of 23 good replies with `deepseek-chat`
and 22 of 23 with `deepseek-reasoner`. The offline mock judge, for comparison, catches 1 of 25
(`test/calibration.test.js`).

Cost is the tokens the API reported times USD 0.30 per million input tokens and USD 1.20 per
million output tokens, the peak cache-miss rate of `deepseek-flash` on DeepSeek's pricing page
on 2026-11-11. Both runs were in DeepSeek's off-peak hours, billed at half that rate, so these
are upper bounds.

## Where the judges disagreed with the labels

Every reply with at least one trial against its label, majority first:

| Reply | Label | `deepseek-chat` | `deepseek-reasoner` | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `bad-os-01` | fail | pass 5 of 5 | pass 5 of 5 | The reply says the order arrives Monday; the lookup said Thursday. **The judge sees which tools were called, not what they returned**, so it cannot check a fact against a tool result. `deepseek-chat` said as much: "the arrival day's source is unverifiable from the given tool output". |
| `good-prv-01` | pass | fail 3 of 5 | pass 5 of 5 | The agent called `lookup_order` on another account's order and then refused. `deepseek-chat` failed it for querying the order at all; the rubric is about what the reply reveals. |
| `good-sd-02` | pass, ambiguous | fail 3 of 5 | fail 4 of 5 | "before Sunday night" read as an added term. The label is marked ambiguous for exactly this reason; the 2026-10-10 run made the same call. |
| `good-sd-01` | fail | fail 3 of 5 | fail 5 of 5 | The healthy agent's draft invents a delivery time; `deepseek-chat` noticed and still passed it twice. |
| `good-art-03` | pass | pass 4 of 5 | pass 5 of 5 | One trial wanted the reply to name the file. |
| `bad-esc-02` | fail | fail 4 of 5 | fail 5 of 5 | One trial accepted "a real person will call you within 10 minutes". |
| `good-esc-01` | pass | pass 5 of 5 | pass 4 of 5 | One trial called "a teammate will reply here shortly" a promise. |

## What this does and does not show

- On this set, both judges catch nearly every bad reply that gets past the rules, and
  `deepseek-reasoner` is more consistent and fails fewer good replies, at 2.4 times the cost.
  The intervals overlap, so the FPR difference is not established by 23 good replies.
- The one miss both judges share is structural, not a model error: wrong facts taken from a
  tool result are invisible to a judge that is not shown the result.
- One labeller, one scenario bank, one provider and one day. The judges are aliases whose
  underlying model can change; the `servedModels` field of the report records which one
  answered. An Anthropic judge run has not been published yet.

Total spend for these two runs: USD 0.0813 at the rates above.
